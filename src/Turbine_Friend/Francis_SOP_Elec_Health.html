Generator Electrical Health & Thermal Protection

Insulation Integrity, Corona Discharge & Heater-Breaker Logic

⚠️ THE INVISIBLE DEGRADATION

Context: Generator windings operate at high voltage (typically 6.6-11 kV for 1.3 MW units). Insulation degradation is gradual and invisible until catastrophic failure occurs.

  • The Risk: Insulation breakdown causes turn-to-turn short circuits → localized overheating → slot discharge → total winding failure.
  • Repair Cost: Re-winding a generator stator costs $50,000-$150,000 and requires 3-6 months downtime.
  • Prevention: Early detection through visual inspection, thermal monitoring, and proper moisture control.
SOP 1: Corona & Slot Discharge Detection

Corona discharge occurs when electrical field stress exceeds air's dielectric strength, ionizing the air and creating ozone.

1.1 The "Ozone Smell Test"

Physics: High-voltage corona produces ozone (O₃), which has a distinctive sharp, chlorine-like smell detectable at very low concentrations (~0.01 ppm).

Procedure:

  1. Generator Running: Unit at >70% load (higher voltage = more pronounced corona)
  2. Access: Approach air outlets from generator cooling fans
  3. Sniff Test: Distinctive ozone smell indicates active corona discharge
  4. Location: If ozone detected, shutdown and inspect to locate source

1.2 Visual Inspection for "White Powder"

Corona Byproduct: Prolonged corona discharge breaks down insulation materials, creating a white/gray powdery residue (ozone-degraded resin).

INSPECTION PROCEDURE (During Shutdown):
1. Remove generator end covers to access stator windings
2. Use flashlight to inspect slot exits and end-winding connections
3. Look for white powder accumulation (especially in corners/crevices)
4. Check for darkened/charred insulation (indicates arcing)

1.3 Common Corona Sources

Location Cause Remedy
Slot exits Sharp edge on slot liner Apply corona-resistant tape (3M 2210) to sharp edges
End-winding connections Loose connection creating air gap Re-tighten bolts, clean contact surfaces
Wedge slots Slot wedge not seated properly Remove and re-seat wedge with proper compression
Cable entries Moisture ingress degrading insulation Seal cable entry with epoxy, verify heaters functional
SOP 2: Heater-Breaker Interlock Logic

Critical Requirement: Generator space heaters MUST activate automatically when main breaker opens to prevent condensation damage.

2.1 The Moisture Threat

Physics: When generator stops, windings cool. If ambient humidity is high (>70%), moisture condenses on cold windings, degrading insulation resistance.

Space Heater Function: Maintains winding temperature 5-10°C above ambient → prevents condensation → preserves insulation integrity.

2.2 Interlock Verification Test

  1. Unit Running: Generator at normal load, space heaters OFF (locked out by breaker auxiliary contact)
  2. Trip Breaker: Manually open main breaker
  3. Immediate Check: Space heater pilot light should illuminate within 5 seconds
  4. Temperature Verification: After 30 minutes, measure winding temperature - should be rising
FAILURE CONSEQUENCE: If heaters fail to activate and generator sits idle for >48 hours in humid conditions, insulation resistance can drop from >100 MΩ to <1 MΩ → UNSAFE TO RESTART without drying process.

2.3 Heater System Maintenance

  • Monthly: Verify heater pilot light illuminates when unit stopped
  • Quarterly: Measure heater current draw (compare to nameplate - should be within 10%)
  • Annually: Test auxiliary contact continuity on main breaker
  • After Extended Outage (>1 week): Measure insulation resistance before restart (Megger test)
SOP 3: Winding Thermal Limits

Generator windings use Class F or Class B insulation. Each class has specific temperature limits that must not be exceeded.

3.1 Insulation Class Reference Table

Insulation Class Continuous Limit Hot Spot Limit Emergency (2hr)
Class B 130°C 145°C 155°C
Class F 155°C 170°C 180°C
TYPICAL 1.3 MW GENERATOR SPECIFICATION:
Insulation Class: F (verify on nameplate)
Rated Stator Temp (RTD): 155°C continuous
Alarm Setpoint: 165°C (10°C below hot spot limit)
Trip Setpoint: 175°C (5°C margin to emergency limit)
Ambient Temperature: Assume 40°C for calculations

3.2 Temperature Rise Budget

Engineering Calculation:

  • Ambient: 40°C (typical powerhouse environment)
  • Allowable Rise: 155°C - 40°C = 115°C
  • At rated load (1.3 MW), temperature rise should be ~100-110°C
  • If rise exceeds 115°C at rated load → cooling system issue or overload

3.3 RTD Sensor Verification

Annual Test:

  1. Compare all stator RTD readings at standstill (should be within 2-3°C of each other)
  2. Compare RTD reading to handheld IR thermometer measurement on accessible winding
  3. If RTD reads >5°C different → sensor drift → replace RTD
SOP 4: Moisture Control & Megger Testing

Objective: Verify insulation resistance before startup after extended outage or suspected moisture ingress.

4.1 Megger Test Procedure

  1. Disconnect Generator: Open main breaker AND disconnect excitation circuit
  2. Ground Resistance Check: Verify proper ground connection (< 1Ω to station ground)
  3. Megger Setup:
    • Test voltage: 500V DC (for 6.6 kV generator) or 1000V DC (for 11 kV generator)
    • Connect positive to phase A, negative to ground
  4. 1-Minute Test: Apply voltage for 60 seconds, record final resistance value
  5. Repeat: Test Phases B and C independently

4.2 Acceptance Criteria

INSULATION RESISTANCE LIMITS:
PASS: > 100 MΩ (excellent condition, safe to start)
WARNING: 10-100 MΩ (marginal - operate heaters 24hr, re-test)
FAIL: < 10 MΩ (moisture contamination - DO NOT START)

If FAIL: Run space heaters continuously for 48-72 hours, re-test daily until >100 MΩ achieved.
Summary Checklist
  • Weekly (during operation): Ozone smell test at generator air outlets
  • Monthly: Verify heater-breaker interlock functional
  • Quarterly: Visual inspection for white powder (corona byproduct)
  • Annually: RTD sensor calibration verification, heater current draw test
  • After extended shutdown (>1 week): Megger test before restart
