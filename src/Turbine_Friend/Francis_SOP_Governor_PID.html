⚙ Governor PID Tuning

Proportional-Integral-Derivative Control System

⚠️ MANDATORY SAFETY PROTOCOL

STOP. READ THIS FIRST.

  • Grid Synchronization: Never adjust PID parameters while the unit is synchronized to the grid without approval from the grid operator.
  • Load Stability: Perform tuning tests at stable load conditions (avoid rapid load changes during testing).
  • Emergency Stop: Ensure the emergency stop button is accessible and functional before beginning tests.
  • Communication: Notify the control room before initiating step response tests.
PID Fundamentals: The Three Controllers

The governor controls the position of the 20 guide vanes to maintain constant turbine speed (typically 500 RPM for a 12-pole generator at 50 Hz).

The Control Loop

Speed Error = Target Speed - Actual Speed
Control Output = (P × Error) + (I × ∫Error dt) + (D × dError/dt)

Proportional (P): The Immediate Response

What it does: Responds proportionally to the current speed error.

  • High P: Fast response, but can cause overshoot and oscillation.
  • Low P: Slow response, but stable.
  • Typical Range: 2.0 - 6.0 (dimensionless gain)

💡 Simple Analogy: Like a driver who steers harder the further off-course they are.

Integral (I): The Steady-State Corrector

What it does: Eliminates steady-state error by accumulating error over time.

  • High I: Fast elimination of offset, but can cause "wind-up" and instability.
  • Low I: Slow correction, but prevents overshoot.
  • Typical Range: 0.5 - 2.0 seconds (integration time)

💡 Simple Analogy: Like a driver who remembers how long they've been off-course and corrects accordingly.

Derivative (D): The Predictive Damper

What it does: Predicts future error based on the rate of change and applies damping.

  • High D: Strong damping, but sensitive to noise (can cause jitter).
  • Low D: Less damping, smoother response.
  • Typical Range: 0.1 - 0.5 seconds (derivative time)

💡 Simple Analogy: Like a driver who anticipates a turn and starts steering before reaching it.

Dead-Band Analysis

What is Dead-Band?

The dead-band is a small speed range (e.g., ±0.1 RPM) where the governor ignores speed deviations and does not move the guide vanes.

Why It Exists

  • Prevent Wear: Constant micro-adjustments cause excessive wear on the servomotor, linkage pins, and guide vane bearings.
  • Reduce Hunting: Without a dead-band, the governor would continuously "hunt" for the exact setpoint, causing oscillations.
  • Noise Immunity: Filters out small speed fluctuations caused by grid noise or sensor jitter.

Dead-Band Configuration

Parameter Typical Value Effect if Too Large Effect if Too Small
Dead-Band ±0.05 - 0.15 RPM Slow response to load changes Excessive wear, hunting
💡 Tech Note: The dead-band we found during the linkage inspection (mechanical "play" in the pins) is a physical dead-band caused by wear. This is different from the electronic dead-band in the governor software. Both must be minimized.
The Stability Test: Step Response

What is a Step Response Test?

A controlled test where the load is suddenly changed by a fixed amount (typically 10% of rated power) to observe how the governor reacts.

Test Procedure

  1. Initial Conditions: Unit synchronized to grid, stable load (e.g., 5.0 MW).
  2. Record Baseline: Monitor speed, guide vane position, and power output for 2 minutes.
  3. Apply Step Change: Increase load setpoint by 10% (e.g., 5.0 MW → 5.5 MW).
  4. Observe Response: Record speed and vane position for 5 minutes.
  5. Analyze: Measure overshoot, settling time, and oscillation frequency.

Ideal Response Characteristics

Metric Target Value Interpretation
Overshoot < 5% Speed should not exceed setpoint by more than 5%
Settling Time < 10 seconds Time to stabilize within ±1% of setpoint
Oscillations < 2 cycles Should dampen quickly without sustained hunting

Anti-Hunting Logic: Diagnosis & Correction

What is Governor Hunting?

Hunting is a continuous oscillation of the guide vanes around the setpoint, causing speed and power to fluctuate.

Symptoms

  • Speed oscillates ±0.2 - 0.5 RPM at a regular frequency (e.g., every 3-5 seconds).
  • Guide vane position oscillates ±1-2% continuously.
  • Audible "clicking" or "tapping" from the servomotor or linkage.
  • Increased wear on linkage pins and bushings.

Root Causes

1. Mechanical Play (Linkage Backlash)

This is the issue we identified during the pin inspection:

  • Worn Pins: Clearance > 0.5 mm between pin and bushing.
  • Loose Connections: Bolts not torqued to specification.
  • Effect: The servomotor moves, but the guide vanes don't respond immediately, causing a delayed feedback loop.

Solution: Replace worn pins and bushings, re-torque all linkage bolts.

2. Excessive Integral Gain (I)

  • Cause: I value too high, causing "integral wind-up".
  • Effect: The controller over-corrects, causing oscillations.

Solution: Reduce I gain by 20-30% and re-test.

3. Insufficient Derivative Damping (D)

  • Cause: D value too low, insufficient damping of oscillations.
  • Effect: System overshoots and oscillates.

Solution: Increase D gain by 10-20% and re-test.

4. Dead-Band Too Small

  • Cause: Dead-band < 0.05 RPM.
  • Effect: Governor responds to noise, causing continuous micro-adjustments.

Solution: Increase dead-band to 0.10 - 0.15 RPM.

Diagnostic Procedure

  1. Mechanical Inspection: Check all linkage pins for play (target < 0.2 mm clearance).
  2. Monitor Oscillation Frequency: Fast oscillations (< 2 sec)=mechanical issue. Slow oscillations (> 5 sec) = PID tuning issue.
  3. Isolate Cause: Temporarily increase dead-band to 0.3 RPM. If hunting stops, the issue is PID tuning. If hunting continues, the issue is mechanical.
PID Tuning Workflow

Step-by-Step Tuning Process

  1. Start with Conservative Values: P=3.0, I=1.0, D=0.2, Dead-Band=0.10 RPM.
  2. Perform Step Response Test: Apply 10% load change, record response.
  3. Adjust P: If response is too slow, increase P by 0.5. If overshoot is excessive, decrease P by 0.5.
  4. Adjust I: If steady-state error persists, increase I by 0.2. If hunting occurs, decrease I by 0.2.
  5. Adjust D: If oscillations are poorly damped, increase D by 0.1. If response is jittery, decrease D by 0.05.
  6. Iterate: Repeat steps 2-5 until optimal response is achieved.
  7. Document: Record final PID values and step response graphs in the unit logbook.
Tuning Goal: Fast Response + Minimal Overshoot + No Hunting
