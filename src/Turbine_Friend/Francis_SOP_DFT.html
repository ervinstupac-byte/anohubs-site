Critical SOP: Draft Tube Air Admission

Vibration Mitigation & Cavitation Buffering

⚠️ THE INVISIBLE ENEMY: CAVITATION

Concept: When water pressure drops below vaporization pressure, bubbles form. When they collapse against metal, they implode with the force of a bullet.

  • The Fix: We cannot stop the physics of low pressure, but we can cushion the blow.
  • Mechanism: Introducing atmospheric air makes the water "spongy" (aerated), absorbing the shockwave of collapsing bubbles.
SOP 1: Vibration & Vortex Control

At partial loads (e.g., 40-60%), a "Rope Vortex" forms in the draft tube, causing severe low-frequency surging.

1.1 The Vacuum Surge

  • Symptom: The powerhouse floor shakes; power output swings (e.g., ±50 kW).
  • Root Cause: An unstable vacuum core in the draft tube center.
  • Mitigation: Passive air valves break this vacuum. By admitting air into the vortex core, the rotation stabilizes, and the "surging" resonance is dampened.
SOP 2: Cleaning Procedure

Air valves only work if they can breathe. Silt and corrosion often seize the check-valves shut.

2.1 Step-by-Step Maintenance

  1. Identify: Locate the 2 or 3 "Snifter Valves" on the Draft Tube cone or Turbine Head Cover.
  2. Disassemble: Unscrew the top cap. Remove the spring and the sealing piston/ball.
  3. Clean:
    • Use a wire brush to remove rust/scale from the seat.
    • Wash the spring in diesel/solvent to ensure free movement.
  4. Test: Push the piston with a finger. It should move smoothly and "spring back" instantly.
  5. Reinstall: Ensure the air mesh filter is clean to prevent debris ingestion.
Visual Check: If the valve makes a "hissing" sound at partial load, it is working correctly. Silence is a warning sign.
SOP 3: Acoustic Diagnostics

The turbine speaks to you. Learn to interpret the sound of the draft tube.

3.1 The "Rock Crushing" Test

  • Sound: Loud cracking, snapping, or stones hitting metal.
  • Diagnosis: Severe Cavitation. The air admission is blocked or insufficient.
  • Action: Force open the air valve. If the sound softens to a "smooth hum" or "white noise", the diagnosis is confirmed: The unit is starving for air.

3.2 The "Chugging" Sound

  • Sound: Rhythmic, deep breathing/gasping (0.5 Hz - 2 Hz).
  • Diagnosis: Draft Tube Vortex resonance.
  • Action: Check if operation is in the "Rough Zone" (typically 30-55% load). Move load up or down to stabilize.
Summary Checklist
  • Weekly: Listen for "Rock Crushing" sounds.
  • Monthly: Check air valves for "hissing" (operation).
  • Quarterly: Disassemble and clean valve springs/seats.
  • Rule: Air is the cheapest spare part—use it generously.
