01 ⚠️ OIL FILM IS THE BEARING

Context: The heavy rotating mass of the turbine/generator floats on a microscopic film of oil.

  • Danger: If oil overheats (> 60°C), viscosity drops. Thin oil cannot carry the shaft.
  • Result: Metal-to-metal contact, catastrophic babbit wiping (melting), and prolonged outage.

Heat is removed from the oil by the cooling water system. In rivers with heavy silt, this is the primary failure point.

02 1.1 Intake Filtration

  1. Check: Inspect mesh filters on cooling water lines daily.
  2. Clean: If differential pressure is high, remove and scrub the mesh. Silt blocks flow.

02 1.2 "Backflush" of Exchangers

Silt accumulates inside heat exchanger tubes, acting as an insulator.

  • Protocol: Close inlet/outlet valves. Open bypass valves for "Backflush" to reverse flow direction for 5 minutes. This ejects deposited sediment.
  • Verification: Check water at discharge. Should transition from muddy to clear.

Oil tells you visually.

03 2.1 Water Contamination

  • Symptom: Oil in sight glass looks "Milky" or cloudy.
  • Cause: Cooling water entering oil chamber (Seal failure or exchanger leak).
  • Action: EMERGENCY STOP. Centrifuge oil or replace. 0.1% water reduces bearing life by 50%.

03 2.2 Vibration Correlation

Note: High temperature is a symptom, not the disease.

  • If cooling is perfect but temperature rises: Check Alignment (Run-Out).
  • Shaft friction creates excess heat that the cooler cannot remove.
