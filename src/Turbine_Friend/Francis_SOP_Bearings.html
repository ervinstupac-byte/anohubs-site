Critical SOP: Main Bearings & Cooling

Thermal Protection & Heat Exchange Logic

⚠️ THE OIL FILM IS THE BEARING

Context: The heavy rotating mass of the turbine/generator floats on a microscopic film of oil.

  • The Danger: If the oil gets too hot (> 60°C), its viscosity drops. Thin oil cannot support the shaft.
  • Result: Metal-to-metal contact, catastrophic wiping of the babbit material, and prolonged shutdown.
SOP 1: Cooling Water Logic

Heat is removed from the oil by the cooling water system. In silt-heavy rivers, this is the primary failure point.

1.1 Intake Filtration

  1. Check: Inspect the mesh filters on the cooling water intake lines daily.
  2. Clean: If pressure differential is high, remove and scrub the mesh. Silt blocks flow.

1.2 Heat Exchanger "Back-Flush"

Silt accumulates inside the heat exchanger coils, acting as an insulator.

  • Protocol: Close the inlet/outlet valves. Open the "Back-Flush" bypass valves to reverse flow direction for 5 minutes. This dislodges settled sediment.
  • Verify: Check the discharge water. It should turn from muddy to clear.
SOP 2: Oil Condition Monitoring

The oil speaks to you visually.

2.1 Water Contamination

  • Symptom: Oil in sight glass looks "Milky" or cloudy (like latte).
  • Cause: Cooling water is entering the oil chamber (Seal failure or heat exchanger leak).
  • Action: IMMEDIATE STOP. Centrifuge oil or replace. 0.1% water reduces bearing life by 50%.

2.2 Vibration Correlation

Note: High temperature is arguably a symptom, not a disease.

  • If cooling is perfect but temperature rises: Check Alignment (Run-Out).
  • Shaft friction creates excess heat that the cooler cannot remove.
SOP 3: Temperature Limits
Condition Temp Range Action
Normal 18°C - 55°C Monitor.
Alarm 60°C inspect Cooling & Oil Level. Cleaning filters.
Trip 70°C EMERGENCY STOP. Do not restart without inspection.
Summary Checklist
  • Daily: Clean cooling water filters.
  • Weekly: "Back-flush" heat exchangers.
  • On Alarm: Check for "Milky" oil immediately.
