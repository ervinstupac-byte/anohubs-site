🔊 Acoustic Diagnostics

The Sensory Analysis Protocol

Using sound signatures to identify internal failures before sensor triggers

⚠️ LISTENING SAFETY PROTOCOL

STOP. READ THIS FIRST.

  • Hearing Protection: Always wear hearing protection when unit is operating. Remove briefly only for diagnostic listening at safe locations.
  • Safe Zones: Never listen from locations with rotating machinery exposure. Use designated listening posts only.
  • Baseline Recording: Record "healthy" sounds during commissioning for future comparison.
The Acoustic Map: Four Technical Zones

Turbine sound analysis is divided into four distinct zones, each with characteristic acoustic signatures.

ZONE A

Intake & Spiral Case

Location: Upstream of the runner, from trash rack through MIV to spiral case entry.

Normal Sounds:

  • "Smooth Rush": Steady water flow sound, like a large river - indicates clean flow path.
  • "Soft Hiss": Minor turbulence at spiral case entry - normal at high flow rates.

Abnormal Sounds:

  • "Clunk" or "Bang": Large debris hitting MIV or trash rack.
    Action: Inspect intake, check trash rack for blockage → Francis_SOP_Intake.html
  • "Whistling" or "Screaming": Partially closed MIV causing high-velocity jet.
    Action: Check MIV position sensor and servomotor.
  • "Gurgling": Air entrainment in spiral case.
    Action: Check upstream water level, inspect MIV seals.
ZONE B

Runner & Guide Vanes

Location: The heart of energy conversion—guide vanes, runner blades, and immediate vicinity.

Normal Sounds:

  • "Deep Hum": Rotational frequency (8.3 Hz for 500 RPM) - indicates smooth operation.
  • "Blade Passage Tone": Number of runner blades × rotational frequency (e.g., 13 blades × 8.3 Hz = 108 Hz).

Abnormal Sounds:

  • "Rock-Crushing" or "Gravel-Dropping": Continuous, violent sound - classic cavitation signature.
    Action: Immediate inspection required → Francis_SOP_Welding.html
  • "Rattling" or "Clattering": Loose runner bolts, damaged blades, or foreign object trapped.
    Action: Stop unit, perform internal inspection.
  • "Grinding" or "Scraping": Runner contact with labyrinth seal or casing.
    Action: Check shaft alignment and labyrinth gaps → Francis_SOP_Precision.html
  • "Popping" (irregular): Guide vane linkage play or mechanical backlash.
    Action: Inspect linkage pins → Francis_SOP_Linkage.html
ZONE C

Draft Tube

Location: Downstream of runner, from draft tube cone to tailrace exit.

Normal Sounds:

  • "Muffled Roar": Turbulent water exiting into tailrace - normal at full load.

Abnormal Sounds:

  • "Thumping" or "Pulsing" (0.2-2 Hz): Low-frequency vortex rope formation.
    Action: Part-load operation issue. Check air admission → Francis_SOP_DFT.html or Francis_SOP_Vortex_Control.html
  • "Surging" (irregular pulsations): Draft tube pressure oscillations causing whole-unit vibration.
    Action: Adjust load or install air admission valve.
ZONE D

Bearings & Generator

Location: All rotating mechanical components outside the water path.

Normal Sounds:

  • "Smooth Whirr": Bearing oil film and shaft rotation - barely audible.
  • "Electrical Hum" (50/60 Hz): Generator magnetic field - consistent tone.

Abnormal Sounds:

  • "High-Pitched Squeal" (metallic): Oil-film collapse, bearing contact with shaft.
    Action: STOP UNIT IMMEDIATELY. Inspect bearing → Francis_SOP_Bearings.html
  • "Rumbling" or "Grinding": Bearing degradation, damaged rolling elements.
    Action: Check bearing temperature, plan replacement.
  • "Crackling" or "Popping" (electrical): Brush sparking, insulation breakdown.
    Action: Inspect brushes → Francis_SOP_Generator.html or Francis_SOP_Elec_Health.html
  • "Varying Hum" (frequency modulation): Rotor eccentricity or magnetic imbalance.
    Action: Perform run-out check, inspect air gap uniformity.
Acoustic Fingerprinting Techniques

Tool Selection

  • Industrial Stethoscope: Best for bearing diagnostics. Place probe on bearing housing to isolate internal sounds.
  • Metal Rod Method: Use a 30-50 cm steel rod. Place one end on the component, hold the other end to your ear (with hearing protection removed briefly). Acts as acoustic waveguide.
  • Sound Level Meter: For quantitative analysis. Record dB levels at fixed locations for trend analysis.
  • Smartphone Apps: Basic spectrum analyzers (FFT) can identify frequency peaks.

Listening Technique

  1. Establish Baseline: Record sounds during commissioning or after major overhaul at various loads (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%).
  2. Systematic Approach: Listen at the same locations during each inspection (e.g., turbine bearing, generator DE/NDE, spiral case access points).
  3. Load Variation: Change load incrementally (±10%) while listening. Abnormal sounds often change with load.
  4. Document: Use voice recorder to capture sounds. Note load, temperature, and vibration levels at time of recording.
Frequency Recognition

Broadband vs. Discrete Tones

Broadband Noise: Wide frequency range with no distinct peaks. Sounds like "white noise" or "rushing water."

  • Cause: General turbulence, water flow, random vibration.
  • Significance: Normal at most operating conditions. Increase indicates flow path blockage or roughness.

Discrete Tones: Narrow frequency peaks that stand out from background noise.

  • Cause: Specific mechanical or hydraulic phenomenon with periodic nature.
  • Examples:
    • Rotational frequency (8.3 Hz for 500 RPM) → Shaft imbalance
    • Blade passage frequency (13 blades × 8.3 Hz = 108 Hz) → Runner blade issues
    • Vortex shedding frequency (0.2-2 Hz) → Draft tube vortex rope
    • High-frequency squeal (> 1000 Hz) → Bearing failure
  • Significance: Discrete tones indicate specific failure modes. Always investigate.

Frequency Calculation Guide

Rotational Frequency (Hz): RPM ÷ 60

Blade Passage Frequency (Hz): (Number of Blades) × (RPM ÷ 60)

Example for 500 RPM, 13-blade runner:

  • Rotational: 500 ÷ 60 = 8.33 Hz
  • Blade Passage: 13 × 8.33 = 108.3 Hz

Inspection Schedule
  • Daily: Listen during routine rounds. Note any new sounds.
  • Weekly: Systematic zone-by-zone acoustic inspection using stethoscope.
  • Monthly: Record sounds at standard load points for trending.
  • After Load Changes: Listen for 5 minutes after significant load change (> 20%).
  • After Maintenance: Establish new baseline after any turbine work.
