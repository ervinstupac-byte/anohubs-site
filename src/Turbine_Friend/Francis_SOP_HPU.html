Critical SOP: HPU & Nitrogen Systems

Ref: 1.3 MW Francis Units | Pressure Hygiene & ESD Protocol

⚠️ HIGH PRESSURE HAZARD

DANGER: Hydraulic oil at 100+ bar is lethal.

  • De-Energize: Before any HPU disassembly, open the manual ball valves to drain the Accumulators back to the tank. Gauge must read 0 bar.
  • Injection Risk: Never use hands to check for leaks. Pinhole leaks can inject oil into the bloodstream.
SOP 1: Nitrogen Accumulator Protocol

The Accumulators are the "Battery" of the safety system. They allow the MIV to close even if the electricity (pumps) fails.

1.1 ESD Energy Verification (Quarterly)

Goal: Ensure sufficient stored energy for Emergency Shut Down.

  1. Isolate and Drain: Isolate one accumulator bank. Drain oil side to tank.
  2. Connect Kit: Attach the Nitrogen Charging Kit to the top bladder valve.
  3. Measure Pre-Charge (P0):
    • P0 should be approx. 90% of Minimum Working Pressure (Pmin).
    • Example: If MIV requires 80 bar to close, P0 should be ~72 bar.
  4. Pass Criteria: Pressure > 90% of Target.
  5. Fail Criteria: Bladder is empty or collapsed. Recharge immediately.
💡 Tech Note: If nitrogen needs frequent recharging, the rubber bladder is likely porous and needs replacement.
SOP 2: Oil Hygiene Standards

Silt is the enemy of hydraulic precision. "Stiction" (Static Friction) in valves causes erratic MIV movement.

2.1 ISO Cleanliness Protocol

  • Oil Type: Use only high-grade L-HL or L-HM hydraulic oil (ISO VG 46/68).
  • Target Cleanliness: maintain ISO 4406 18/16/13 or better.
  • Sampling: Take samples from the reservoir middle zone (not bottom sludge) annually.

2.2 High-Pressure Filter Replacement

  1. Inspect the "Clogging Indicator" on the pressure filter housing daily.
  2. Red Flag = Stop & Replace. Do not bypass.
  3. Procedure:
    • Switch to duplex standby filter (if available) or shut down HPU.
    • Vent pressure. unscrew bowl.
    • Install new cartridge (Do not clean disposable elements).
    • Check O-ring condition.
SOP 3: Water Hammer & Stroke Timing

Dirty oil leads to valve sticking. If a valve sticks and then suddenly releases, the MIV may slam shut.

3.1 The Joukowsky Equation (Water Hammer)

ΔP = ρ · c · Δv
  • ΔP: Pressure Rise (The "Hammer").
  • ρ (Rho): Density of water.
  • c: Celerity (Speed of sound in water ~1200 m/s).
  • Δv: Change in velocity (Stopping the water).

3.2 The Hygiene Link

Scenario: Silt causes the proportional valve to stick. The MIV doesn't move for 5 seconds, then snaps shut in 1 second.

  • Result: $\Delta v$ becomes instantaneous. $\Delta P$ spikes to 50+ bar.
  • Consequence: Penstock rupture or spiral case fracture.
  • Prevention: Clean oil ensures smooth, linear valve actuation (e.g., controlled 40-second closure).
Summary Checklist
  • Quarterly: Check Nitrogen Pre-Charge.
  • Daily: Check Filter Clogging Indicators.
  • Annually: Lab analysis of L-HM Oil (ISO 4406).
  • Safety: Never work on pressurized accumulators.
