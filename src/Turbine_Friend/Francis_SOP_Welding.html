🔥 Cavitation Repair Welding

Stainless Steel Runner Restoration Protocol

⚠️ MANDATORY SAFETY PROTOCOL

STOP. READ THIS FIRST.

  • Hot Work Permit: Obtain permit before welding. Fire extinguishers nearby.
  • Ventilation: Stainless steel welding produces hexavalent chromium fumes (carcinogenic). Use forced ventilation and respirators.
  • Eye Protection: Welding helmet with shade 10-12 lens.
  • Electrical Safety: Ensure welding machine is grounded. Dry work area.
Material Match: Electrode Selection

Why Material Matching Matters

The runner is typically 13Cr4Ni Martensitic Stainless Steel. Wrong electrode causes galvanic corrosion, cracking, or reduced hardness.

Recommended Electrodes

  • 308L (E308L-16): General cavitation repair. Austenitic, good corrosion resistance.
  • 309L (E309L-16): Dissimilar metal joints. Higher chromium content.
💡 Tech Note: 308L or 309L are preferred—NO preheating or post-weld heat treatment required.

Welding Parameters

  • Current: 80-120 A (3.2 mm electrode)
  • Polarity: DCEP
  • Interpass Temperature: < 150°C
Profile Template: Preserving Blade Curvature

Creating a Profile Gauge

  1. Select Undamaged Reference: Find adjacent undamaged blade.
  2. Material: Thick cardboard, plastic, or thin aluminum (1-2 mm).
  3. Trace Profile: Press material against blade, trace exact curvature.
  4. Cut Template: Cut along traced line.
  5. Label: Mark blade number and position.
[Visualization: Profile Gauge Template]
Template → Trace → Cut → Verify repaired blade

Using the Template

  1. After grinding: Remove all damaged material.
  2. After welding: Build up 1-2 mm excess weld metal.
  3. Final grinding: Use template to guide grinding—blade must match template exactly.
⚠️ CRITICAL: Even 1 mm deviation reduces turbine efficiency by 1-2%.
The 'Smoothness' Standard: Mirror Finish

Why Surface Finish Matters

Any surface roughness acts as a nucleation site for new cavitation bubbles.

Target: Ra < 1.6 μm (Mirror Finish)

Grinding and Polishing Procedure

  1. Coarse Grinding (40-60 grit): Remove excess weld, shape to profile.
  2. Medium Grinding (80-120 grit): Smooth coarse marks.
  3. Fine Grinding (180-240 grit): Achieve smooth surface.
  4. Polishing (320-600 grit): Polish to mirror finish.
💡 Tech Note: Always grind parallel to water flow direction (leading to trailing edge). Cross-grain scratches create turbulence.

Visual Inspection

  • Mirror Test: Flashlight at shallow angle should reflect clearly.
  • Fingernail Test: No ridges or scratches should be felt.
Dynamic Balance Warning

Why Balance Matters

Adding weld metal to one blade creates mass imbalance, causing vibration, bearing wear, shaft fatigue, and seal failure.

Weld Metal → Mass Imbalance → Centrifugal Force → Vibration

Mitigation Strategies

1. Symmetric Repair (Preferred)

Repair all damaged blades to maintain symmetry.

2. Counterweight Addition

Add same mass to opposite blade (180° away) in non-critical area.

3. Post-Repair Run-Out Check

  1. Mount runner on balancing stand.
  2. Rotate slowly, measure radial run-out with dial indicator.
  3. Target: Run-out < 0.05 mm at blade tips.
⚠️ CRITICAL: DO NOT add more than 100g weld to single blade without balancing specialist consultation.
Materials & Tools Checklist
  • Electrodes: E308L-16 or E309L-16 (3.2 mm).
  • Welding: SMAW welder, 80-120 A.
  • Grinding: Angle grinder, flap discs (40-600 grit).
  • Template: Cardboard, plastic, or aluminum.
  • Measurement: Calipers, dial indicator.
  • Safety: Welding helmet, respirator (P100), gloves, fire extinguisher.
