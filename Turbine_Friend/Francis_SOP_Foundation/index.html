SOP: Foundation Integrity

🔨 Foundation Integrity

Structural Stability & Grout Inspection Protocol

⚠️ MANDATORY SAFETY PROTOCOL

STOP. READ THIS FIRST.

  • Unit Shutdown: Foundation inspection requires unit shutdown and mechanical isolation.
  • Heavy Lifting: If anchor bolt replacement is needed, heavy lifting equipment will be required. Ensure proper rigging certification.
  • Chemical Hazards: Epoxy resins contain volatile organic compounds (VOCs). Use in well-ventilated areas with respirators.
  • Confined Space: Turbine pit may be classified as confined space. Follow entry protocols.
The 'Hollow' Test: Acoustic Grout Inspection

Why Foundation Integrity Matters

The turbine and generator are mounted on a concrete foundation with grout filling the gap between the equipment feet and the floor. If the grout delaminates or develops air pockets:

  • Vibration Amplification: Loss of stiffness allows vibration to increase.
  • Misalignment: Equipment can shift, causing shaft misalignment.
  • Stress Concentration: Uneven support creates high stress points on the frame.
  • Bearing Failure: Misalignment accelerates bearing wear.

The Hammer Test Procedure

  1. Select Hammer: Use a small ball-peen hammer (200-300g) or a geologist's rock hammer.
  2. Identify Test Areas: Focus on grout around turbine feet, generator feet, and anchor bolt plates.
  3. Tap Systematically: Tap the grout surface in a grid pattern (every 10-15 cm).
  4. Listen for Sound:
    • Solid Grout: High-pitched, sharp "ting" sound.
    • Delaminated/Hollow: Low-pitched, dull "thud" or "hollow" sound.
  5. Mark Defects: Use chalk to mark all hollow-sounding areas.
[Visualization: Hammer Test]
Solid Grout: "TING!" (High pitch)
Hollow/Delaminated: "THUD" (Low pitch, hollow echo)
💡 Tech Note: The hollow sound indicates air pockets or separation between the grout and the concrete floor. This is often caused by shrinkage during curing, vibration-induced cracking, or poor initial installation.

Documentation

  • Sketch a map of the turbine/generator foundation.
  • Mark all hollow areas with dimensions.
  • Take photos of marked areas.
  • Estimate total affected area (m²).
Anchor Bolt Tension: Re-Torquing Protocol

Why Anchor Bolts Loosen

Anchor bolts hold the turbine and generator to the foundation. Over time, they can loosen due to:

  • Vibration: Continuous vibration causes micro-movements that loosen nuts.
  • Thermal Cycling: Temperature changes cause expansion/contraction.
  • Grout Settlement: If grout settles or cracks, bolt tension is lost.

Inspection Procedure

  1. Locate All Anchor Bolts: Typically 8-16 bolts per unit (4-8 for turbine, 4-8 for generator).
  2. Visual Inspection: Check for rust, corrosion, bent threads, or missing nuts.
  3. Initial Torque Check: Use a calibrated torque wrench to check current torque.
  4. Record Values: Document current torque for each bolt.

Re-Torquing Procedure

Torque = K × D × P
K = Friction factor (0.15-0.20)
D = Bolt diameter (mm)
P = Desired preload (kN)
  1. Determine Target Torque: Consult OEM manual. Typical values:
    • M24 bolts: 400-600 Nm
    • M30 bolts: 800-1200 Nm
    • M36 bolts: 1400-2000 Nm
  2. Clean Threads: Wire brush all threads and apply anti-seize compound.
  3. Tighten in Star Pattern: Tighten bolts in a star/cross pattern to distribute load evenly.
  4. Multi-Pass Tightening:
    • Pass 1: Tighten to 50% of target torque.
    • Pass 2: Tighten to 75% of target torque.
    • Pass 3: Tighten to 100% of target torque.
  5. Final Verification: Re-check all bolts to ensure torque is maintained.
⚠️ CRITICAL: Over-torquing can strip threads or crack the foundation. Under-torquing allows movement. Use a calibrated torque wrench and follow OEM specifications exactly.
Epoxy Injection: Crack Repair & Grout Restoration

When to Use Epoxy Injection

Epoxy injection is used to fill foundation cracks and restore grout integrity when:

  • Hollow areas are detected by hammer test.
  • Visible cracks in grout or concrete (width > 0.5 mm).
  • Grout has delaminated from the foundation.

Epoxy Selection

Type: Use structural epoxy resin designed for concrete repair (e.g., Sikadur, Hilti HIT-RE 500).

Properties:

  • Low viscosity (for deep penetration into cracks)
  • High compressive strength (> 70 MPa)
  • Good adhesion to concrete and steel
  • Low shrinkage during curing

Injection Procedure

  1. Prepare Surface:
    • Clean all loose material, dust, and oil from cracks.
    • Use compressed air to blow out debris.
    • Wipe with acetone to remove oil/grease.
  2. Drill Injection Ports:
    • Drill holes (10-12 mm diameter) at 15-30 cm intervals along the crack.
    • Drill at 45° angle into the crack.
    • Clean holes with compressed air.
  3. Install Injection Packers:
    • Insert plastic or metal packers into drilled holes.
    • Seal around packers with fast-setting epoxy putty.
  4. Mix Epoxy:
    • Follow manufacturer's mixing ratio exactly (typically 2:1 or 3:1 resin:hardener).
    • Mix thoroughly for 2-3 minutes.
    • Use within pot life (typically 20-40 minutes).
  5. Inject Epoxy:
    • Use a manual or pneumatic injection pump.
    • Start at the lowest point of the crack.
    • Inject slowly until epoxy flows from the next packer.
    • Move to next packer and repeat.
    • Continue until entire crack is filled.
  6. Curing:
    • Allow epoxy to cure per manufacturer's spec (typically 24-72 hours).
    • Do not disturb or load during curing.
  7. Finishing:
    • Remove packers after curing.
    • Grind flush any excess epoxy.
    • Patch packer holes with epoxy putty.
[Visualization: Epoxy Injection Process]
Crack → Drill Ports → Install Packers → Inject Epoxy → Cure → Finish
💡 Tech Note: For large hollow areas (> 0.5 m²), consider removing and replacing the grout entirely rather than injection. Epoxy is expensive and best suited for cracks and small voids.
Inspection Schedule
  • Annual Inspection: Hammer test all grout areas during scheduled outage.
  • Bi-Annual Torque Check: Verify anchor bolt torque every 2 years.
  • Post-Vibration Event: If abnormal vibration is detected, immediately inspect foundation and anchor bolts.
  • After Seismic Activity: Inspect foundation after any earthquake (magnitude > 4.0).
Materials & Tools Checklist
  • Hammer Test: Ball-peen hammer (200-300g), chalk for marking.
  • Torque Tools: Calibrated torque wrench (0-2000 Nm range), socket set.
  • Epoxy Injection: Structural epoxy resin, injection pump, packers, drill, mixing paddles.
  • Cleaning: Wire brush, compressed air, acetone.
  • Safety: Respirator (for epoxy fumes), gloves, safety glasses.
  • Documentation: Camera, foundation sketch/map, inspection log.
